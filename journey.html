Your Journey

From your first night to your own front door

Some people arrive in crisis. Some arrive exhausted. Some arrive not quite believing that this time might be different. Wherever you're starting from, you won't be walking it on your own.

This isn't a programme you get enrolled onto. There's no course to complete and no timetable to keep up with. It's a path that unfolds at whatever pace suits you — five stages, and you move between them when you're ready, not when a calendar says so.

The Five Stages

What to Expect

1

Arrival

Your first few days are about one thing: feeling safe.

We won't rush you into assessments or paperwork. We'll welcome you, show you your room, introduce you to the house, and give you space to breathe. For a lot of people who come to us, this is the first time in a long while they've had a door they can close and a bed that's their own.

  • A proper conversation within your first 48 hours — not a form-filling exercise, just someone asking where you are and what you need
  • A support plan you build together with your keyworker, in your words
  • Time to settle — getting to know the house and the people in it
2

Building Skills

Once you feel settled, the real work begins — at your pace, not ours.

For some people that means learning to cook a meal or manage a budget. For others it means facing the things that led to losing their home in the first place. We won't judge you for where you're starting. What we care about is where you're heading.

  • Regular sessions with your keyworker, focused on your goals
  • Practical skills — cooking, cleaning, budgeting, managing bills
  • Emotional support — access to counselling, recovery support, rebuilding your confidence
  • Work, training and education — when you're ready, and not before
3

Preparing to Move On

Moving on isn't something that happens to you. It's something you choose, when you're ready.

This stage starts when you and your keyworker agree that living independently is a realistic next step. It's about preparation, not pressure. We want you to walk into your own place feeling capable — not pushed out before your time.

  • Planning the move — what kind of place suits you, what area, what support you might still want
  • Your housing options — private rented, social housing, supported move-on
  • Tenancy skills — understanding your agreement, knowing your rights
  • Building the confidence to live on your own, because skills without confidence aren't enough
4

Moving Out

This is the moment everything has been building toward.

Moving into your own home is a huge milestone. If you've been through homelessness, abuse, or prison, it can also be frightening. You won't face that day on your own.

  • Practical help with the move — transport, furniture, the things you need
  • Getting set up — utilities, broadband, council tax
  • Celebrating it, because it matters, and you deserve to hear that
5

Aftercare

Our job isn't finished when you move out.

The first few weeks of living independently are the hardest. Most providers close the file when someone leaves. We don't. If you're struggling with a first council tax bill, or you're behind on rent in month one, or living alone is simply overwhelming — there's still someone to ring.

  • A settling-in check in your first week
  • Help with bills — council tax, utilities, working out what's due and when
  • Budgeting support as you manage on your own
  • Regular check-ins to catch problems early
  • You can still contact us after you've moved on
  • Contact reduces gradually, as your confidence grows — until you don't need us
How Much Support

As Much As You Need, For As Long As You Need It

We use three broad levels to plan our time. They're not boxes you get put in — they simply help us make sure the people who need the most attention get it. You can move between them in either direction, at any time.

🌱

Early Days

Around 4–7 hours a week

For when you've just arrived, you're going through something difficult, or things feel like a lot. You'll see your keyworker several times a week, with hands-on help and someone alongside you for appointments.

🌿

Building Stability

Around 2–4 hours a week

For when you're getting there but still working things through — rebuilding routines, sorting out health, learning to manage money. A weekly session plus help where you want it.

🌳

Settling In Well

Up to 2 hours a week

For when you're managing day to day and growing in confidence, but you like knowing someone's there. A weekly check-in, help keeping benefits straight, and planning your move on when it feels right.

Those hours are a planning guide, not a limit and not an entitlement. What matters is that you get what you need — and that you never feel watched, managed, or processed. This is your home. We want it to feel like one.

Day to Day

What a Normal Week Looks Like

🏡

A House Meeting

Once a week everyone in the house gets together for about half an hour. It's informal — how the house is running, anything practical, and a chance to check in with each other.

☕

Time With Your Keyworker

One person who knows you and your situation. Two or three times a week when you've just moved in; a quick weekly catch-up once you're settled. It's your call as much as ours.

🚪

An Open Door

Life doesn't happen on a schedule. If you need help with a benefits letter, want someone with you at the GP, or just need to talk something through — we're there. It's often the most useful thing we do.

Specialist Support

If You Need More Than Housing Support

Some things need specialist help. We will connect you with the people who provide it.

🛡️

If You're Leaving Abuse

Help connecting with specialist domestic abuse services for safety planning, an IDVA and legal protection. We understand the courage it takes to leave. You'll be believed, respected, and safe here.

🌤️

If You're in Recovery

Relapse prevention, help getting to peer support and mutual aid groups, and a house that supports your recovery instead of undermining it. Recovery isn't a straight line. A setback won't be held against you.

🔑

If You've Left Prison

Help meeting your licence conditions, liaison with probation, and a structured resettlement. Leaving prison without a stable address makes everything harder. This is somewhere to build from.

🧭

If You've Left Care

Transition support, independent living skills, and planning what comes next. Leaving care often means no safety net. We'll be that while you find your feet.

When you no longer need us, you haven't left. You've finished.

That's what this whole path is built to do. If you'd like to talk it through — for yourself or for someone you're supporting — we're here.
